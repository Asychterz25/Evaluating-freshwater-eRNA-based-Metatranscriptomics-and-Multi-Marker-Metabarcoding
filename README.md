# Evaluating eRNA-based Metatranscriptomics and Multi-Marker Metabarcoding for Freshwater Community Assessment

1 Department of Biology, McGill University, Montreal, Quebec, Canada

# Abstract
Central to accurately identifying aquatic communities is the methodology deployed, yet the performance of emerging eRNA-based approaches remains poorly understood. Although metabarcoding has proven to be an effective method, it is subject to well-known limitations, such as primer-affinity bias which limits community resolution. Metatranscriptomics is gaining attention as both a method to characterize gene expression and a primer-free approach for community identification. However, its high sequencing-depth requirements and demanding workflows compared to metabarcoding, have limited its routine use. Likewise, the benefits of using eRNA for these molecular methods are only beginning to be understood, and its performance is not well characterized. Here, we evaluated how eRNA multi-marker metabarcoding (18S, COI, 23S), and eRNA metatranscriptomics capture eukaryotic planktonic community responses across moderate and high nutrient enrichment and realistic temperature conditions in experimental mesocosms. All molecular approaches detected significant community shifts in response to nutrient enrichment and showed broad similarity in dominant taxa at high taxonomic ranks. However, molecular methods identified contrary nutrient enrichment driven effects on within group similarity. Furthermore, metabarcoding failed to identify several abundant zooplankton, such as Bosminidae, that were detected by metatranscriptomics and morphological analysis. These results indicate that method-specific biases influence ecological conclusions, with multi-marker metabarcoding emerging as a robust alternative and metatranscriptomics showing promise but database limitations prevent its widespread adoption.
